Research results

Key findings

  • Observation. Energy-only learners survived in 0/50 seed/ratio runs; with the kin-weighted reward, 18/50 survived, and 17/20 at B/C ≥ 4 (Fisher p = 2.6e-08).
  • Observation. Untrained networks with the same founders survived in 10/10 seeds at B/C = 4, so learning with the energy-only reward removed cooperation that was already present.
  • Conclusion. Survival alone does not need kin information: paying for every gift kept 10/10 seeds alive at B/C = 4, versus 7/10 with the kin-weighted reward.
  • Observation. The kin-weighted reward improved targeting: compared with a kin-blind reward of the same size, the share of gifts satisfying rB > C rose by +0.098 (8/10 seeds, p = 0.0098).
  • Conclusion. Kin information changes whom agents help more than whether the population survives. Learners did not learn to use behavioural history, and they kept feeding genetic mimics.

Every number in this section is generated by frontend/export_web_data.py from results/study_b_hamilton/analysis.json and results/study_c_robustness/analysis.json. To check them against the models yourself, use Reproduce a result above, which reruns the saved checkpoints in your browser.

What kin information changes is whom agents help: inclusive minus kin-blind, share of gifts with rB > C: +0.098 (8/10 seeds, sign-flip p = 0.0098); kin preference: +0.038 (8/10 seeds, sign-flip p = 0.025).
